Why an employer commissions 1:1 support

Because the issue is individual — and a generic wellbeing resource will not always address it.

An employer may notice reduced focus, withdrawal, irritability, hesitation, lower confidence, absence or increasing manager involvement. 1:1 support gives that employee somewhere private to work on the actual issue rather than receiving the same advice as everybody else.

Protect focus and mental capacityWork directly with worry, overthinking and emotional overload that can consume attention during the working day.
Rebuild confidenceHelp employees respond more effectively to setbacks, uncertainty, difficult conversations and situations that have reduced self-belief.
Improve emotional regulationCreate more choice in how the employee responds to triggers and pressure instead of repeatedly falling into the same reaction.
Reduce manager dependenceGive managers somewhere appropriate to signpost instead of becoming the employee's sole emotional support.

How access works

Private for the employee. Defined for the employer.

Where self-booking is selected, employees choose an available appointment directly. Remote sessions are delivered by Google Meet with daytime and selected evening availability; the latest remote appointment starts at 7:45pm. Employees book one appointment at a time, with a maximum of one appointment per week.

What you can measure

You can assess whether the service is being used without knowing what an employee said.

Reporting can be agreed around overall take-up, attendance, no-show patterns and broad non-identifying themes where appropriate. This gives the employer visibility of the provision while preserving the trust that makes confidential support usable.
