What an employer should gain

A clearer route for problems that otherwise drift into absence, performance or management time.

The aim is not to promise a fixed reduction in sickness absence. It is to improve the conditions around early help: access, trust, practical support, clear signposting and an agreed route for employees who need more than a normal management conversation.

Earlier interventionEmployees can access support while still working, rather than only after the situation has escalated.
Reduced manager burdenManagers remain supportive but have somewhere professional to signpost issues beyond their role or competence.
Better employee accessRemote, on-site and group options reduce practical barriers and make the support route easier to understand.
Stronger confidence in confidentialityEmployees know what remains private, what the exceptions are and what information the organisation may receive.
More useful organisational visibilityAppropriate utilisation and broad non-identifying themes can support review without exposing private conversations.
A service that complements existing provisionDesigned to sit alongside HR, Occupational Health, EAP and clinical pathways rather than duplicate them.

Why this matters to managers

Managers need somewhere appropriate to signpost — not another responsibility to absorb.

CIPD guidance published in 2026 reports that line managers take primary responsibility for managing short-term absence in 70% of organisations. A defined wellbeing route can help managers stay within their role while still responding constructively when an employee is struggling.

Source: CIPD Short-term absence guide, 2026.

How we would review the provision

Measure what can be measured without breaching employee trust.

A professional service should give the organisation enough information to judge use and fit, while protecting the content of individual conversations.

UtilisationHow much of the agreed appointment or workshop capacity is being used?
Attendance and no-showsAre employees engaging with the access route once they book?
Access patternsWhich delivery routes are being used most: remote, on-site or group?
Broad non-identifying themesWhere appropriate and safe, recurring themes can help the organisation understand the types of pressure employees are bringing.
Employee feedbackAnonymous or agreed feedback can help assess whether the service feels accessible, relevant and useful.
Fit with existing provisionReview whether the service is filling a genuine gap or duplicating another route employees already use effectively.
A strong wellbeing offer should support employees and make practical sense to the people commissioning it.
What good commissioning avoids

Provision that exists but employees do not use.

The CIPD's 2025 report found that 64% of organisations were taking steps to identify and/or reduce stress, yet only 50% believed those efforts were effective. The lesson is straightforward: having a wellbeing offer is not the same as having an effective one.

The service therefore needs to be understandable, accessible, trusted and relevant to the workforce.

Source: CIPD Health and Wellbeing at Work 2025.
