What I would want to know as the employer

What changes after we commission this?

The service should not be judged by the number of pages in a wellbeing strategy. It should be judged by whether employees can access useful support, whether problems are addressed earlier and whether the organisation has a clearer route for pressures that would otherwise land with managers, HR or absence processes.

Better access to supportEmployees have a clear route into 1:1 help, on-site appointments or practical workshops.
Earlier interventionSupport becomes available before the employee reaches prolonged absence or crisis.
Less management burdenLine managers can signpost rather than trying to personally solve complex emotional issues.
Stronger employee experienceEmployees can see that the organisation has invested in a practical, accessible support route.
Clearer governanceConfidentiality, safeguarding, escalation and reporting boundaries are agreed before delivery begins.
Useful organisational visibilityAppropriate utilisation and broad non-identifying themes can support review without exposing private conversations.

Commissioning process

Clear enough for governance. Flexible enough for real workplaces.

We agree the problem, delivery model, access route and professional boundaries before the service starts. This keeps the employee experience simple while giving the organisation a defined structure.

1

Clarify the workforce need
What is happening, who is affected and what should improve?

2

Select the delivery model
1:1 support, clinics, workshops or a combination.

3

Agree governance
Confidentiality, safeguarding, escalation, data and reporting.

4

Launch and communicate
Employees understand what the service is and how to use it.

5

Review appropriate information
Utilisation, attendance and agreed non-identifying themes.

A strong wellbeing offer should support employees and make practical sense to the people commissioning it.
What good commissioning avoids

Provision that exists but employees do not use.

The CIPD's 2025 report found that 64% of organisations were taking steps to identify and/or reduce stress, yet only 50% believed those efforts were effective. The lesson is straightforward: having a wellbeing offer is not the same as having an effective one.

The service therefore needs to be understandable, accessible, trusted and relevant to the workforce.

Source: CIPD Health and Wellbeing at Work 2025.
