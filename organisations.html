The employer business case

Employee wellbeing becomes a business issue the moment it changes how somebody works.

You do not have to wait for sickness absence to see the effect. Stress, anxiety, emotional overload and personal pressure can show up as slower decisions, reduced concentration, lower confidence, disengagement, presenteeism and more management time.

NHS workforce evidence — 2025

One of the UK's largest workforces shows why attendance alone is not enough.

The 2025 NHS Staff Survey received responses from more than 750,000 trust staff. It shows substantial levels of stress, burnout and working while not feeling well enough to perform duties.

42.36% felt unwell because of work-related stressin the previous 12 months.
56.01% worked while not feeling well enoughto perform their duties in the previous three months.
31.47% often or always felt burnt outbecause of their work.
43.03% felt worn out at the end of the day or shiftoften or always.

Where the employer feels the impact

The cost is often distributed across the working day rather than appearing on one budget line.

Mental and emotional strain can affect output even when attendance appears normal. The operational impact may be seen in performance, decision-making, communication, error risk, team capacity and manager time.

PresenteeismAn employee is at work but functioning below their normal level. Government analysis estimates this costs UK employers around £21bn annually.
Reduced cognitive capacityWorry, rumination and emotional overload compete for attention and can make prioritising, problem-solving and decision-making harder.
Management timeManagers can spend increasing amounts of time holding support conversations or trying to manage issues outside their training.
Team spilloverColleagues can absorb extra workload, disrupted communication and emotional pressure when one person is struggling.
Absence and delayed recoveryStress and mental ill health remain major causes of long-term and short-term absence.
Retention riskWhen problems become entrenched, the employer can ultimately face capability, redeployment, recruitment and replacement costs.
What effective early support can provide

Give employees earlier support, helping them stay functioning and supported at work.

The government's Healthy Working Lifecycle distinguishes between people who are healthy in work, unwell in work, absent and returning, and those moving towards redeployment or exit. The commercial opportunity is clear: give people an effective route into support while there is still time to stabilise functioning.

The Keep Britain Working technical note models benefits for workplace health provision overall. Those national estimates are not presented as guaranteed outcomes of this specific service.

Normal workplace performance.

What better-functioning staff can improve

The benefit is practical, not cosmetic.

01

Focus & decision-making

Reducing the mental load created by anxiety, overthinking and emotional overwhelm can free more attention for the actual job.

02

Confidence & communication

Employees who feel more secure and regulated are better placed to contribute, communicate and handle challenge.

03

Manager capacity

A clear professional signposting route can reduce the expectation that managers personally carry complex employee problems.

04

Attendance stability

Earlier help creates another opportunity to address difficulties before they become more entrenched or disruptive.

05

Employee experience

Support is more credible when employees can see how to access it, understand confidentiality and receive something relevant to their actual problem.

06

Retention

Supporting valued people through periods of difficulty can be less disruptive than allowing problems to progress towards capability or exit.
