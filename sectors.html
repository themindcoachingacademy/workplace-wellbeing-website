Sector use cases

The right wellbeing model depends on how your workforce actually works.

Shift patterns, workforce size, geography, confidentiality concerns, existing HR provision and operational pressure all change what good employee support should look like.

Different workforce, different modelUse the delivery route that removes the biggest barrier
How the need changes by setting

Do not commission the same solution for every workforce.

01

SMEs

Typical need: useful employee support without building a large internal wellbeing function.

Strong fit: remote 1:1 access plus targeted workshops when a shared issue emerges.

02

Private Employers

Typical need: complement existing HR or EAP provision and give employees another trusted route into support.

Strong fit: blended 1:1, clinics and workshops.

03

Healthcare

Typical need: support employees in emotionally demanding environments where pressure can accumulate while people continue working.

Strong fit: confidential 1:1 support and on-site clinics.

04

NHS

Typical need: independent early support that complements Occupational Health, EAP and internal staff-wellbeing pathways.

Strong fit: defined pilot, on-site clinic or remote employee support model.

05

Public Sector

Typical need: visible employee support with clear governance, confidentiality and reporting boundaries.

Strong fit: structured commissioning with agreed utilisation reporting.

06

Distributed Teams

Typical need: consistent access across locations without forcing employees to travel.

Strong fit: UK-wide remote 1:1 support and remote workshops.

The decision framework

Match the service to the barrier.

If employees need privacy, prioritise 1:1 access. If the barrier is visibility and convenience, bring support on-site. If the challenge is broad and shared, use workshops to build prevention at scale.

1

Privacy barrier?
Use independent 1:1 support.

2

Access barrier?
Use on-site wellbeing clinics.

3

Capability gap?
Use workplace workshops.

4

Multiple needs?
Combine the services into one defined model.
