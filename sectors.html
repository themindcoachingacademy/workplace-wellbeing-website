Employer use cases

The right wellbeing model depends on how your workforce actually works.

Shift patterns, workforce size, geography, confidentiality concerns, existing HR provision and operational pressure all change what useful employee support should look like.

How the employer priority changes

The same service should not be sold to every employer in the same way.

The value comes from solving the barrier that matters most in that setting: access, privacy, capacity, governance, reach or consistency.

01

SMEs

Employer priority: give staff access to professional support without creating a large internal wellbeing function.

Likely model: remote 1:1 access with targeted workshops when a shared issue emerges.

Benefit: useful support with low administrative complexity.

02

Private Employers

Employer priority: improve access and complement HR, EAP or existing wellbeing provision.

Likely model: blended 1:1 support, clinics and workshops according to workforce demand.

Benefit: a flexible extra layer rather than a replacement programme.

03

Healthcare Employers

Employer priority: support people working under emotional and operational pressure while protecting confidentiality.

Likely model: confidential 1:1 support and on-site clinics.

Benefit: easier access for staff who may continue working while carrying significant pressure.

04

NHS Employers

Employer priority: provide independent early support that fits alongside Occupational Health, EAP and internal staff-wellbeing routes.

Likely model: defined pilot, on-site clinic or remote employee support model.

Benefit: an additional confidential route that can be reviewed without duplicating clinical pathways.

05

Public-Sector Employers

Employer priority: clear governance, defined scope and appropriate employer visibility.

Likely model: structured commissioning with agreed access, safeguarding and utilisation reporting.

Benefit: greater clarity for procurement, HR and employees using the service.

06

Distributed Employers

Employer priority: consistent access across locations without relying on local providers.

Likely model: UK-wide remote 1:1 support and remote workshops.

Benefit: one support route across multiple sites or home-working teams.

The decision framework

Match the service to the barrier.

If employees need privacy, prioritise 1:1 access. If the barrier is visibility and convenience, bring support on-site. If the challenge is broad and shared, use workshops to build prevention at scale.

Questions worth answering before commissioning

The best model becomes clearer when the operating context is clear.

Where are employees based?Single site, multiple sites, remote, hybrid or shift-based work changes the access model.
What already exists?Existing EAP, Occupational Health, HR or clinical routes should be complemented rather than duplicated.
What stops people using current support?Confidentiality concerns, visibility, waiting time, travel, awareness or lack of relevance may be the real barrier.
Is the need individual or widespread?Individual problems favour 1:1 support; shared patterns may justify group education and prevention.
What governance is required?Public, healthcare and larger employers may require clearer reporting, escalation and data-handling arrangements.
What would success look like?Define the practical outcome before selecting the service: access, engagement, capability, earlier support or manager signposting.
