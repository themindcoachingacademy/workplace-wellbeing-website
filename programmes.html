How programmes work

A clear structure with room to tailor.

A one-off session can introduce useful techniques. A planned series offers opportunities to revisit skills, adapt topics to emerging needs and give employees time to try the learning between sessions.

01

Define the priorities

Discuss the audience, challenges, objectives and what is already available to employees. Identify practical topics that complement existing provision.

02

Agree the schedule

Choose a short workshop series, monthly sessions, quarterly learning or multi-site delivery. We agree frequency, group size, delivery and fees before booking.

03

Deliver and review

Employees practise relevant tools. Review participation, anonymous feedback and useful follow-on topics without making unsupported outcome guarantees.

Programme options

Choose an approach that fits your workforce.

Three-session foundations

A focused sequence around recognising pressure, practising more useful responses and applying them at work. Topics can be adapted to your teams.

Suitable for: an introductory programme or a defined team need.

Monthly wellbeing series

One session each month covering complementary themes such as stress, overthinking, confidence, communication and emotional regulation.

Suitable for: regular staff development and continuing engagement.

Quarterly or multi-site delivery

Periodic workshops across teams or locations, with consistent core learning and space for local priorities.

Suitable for: employers coordinating wellbeing provision across departments or sites.

These are illustrative programme structures, not fixed-price packages. Dates, duration, capacity and fees are confirmed through an agreed proposal.

Responsible workplace wellbeing

Individual skills support — not a substitute for fixing work-related stressors.

Practical employee wellbeing learning can complement your existing people, Occupational Health and wellbeing arrangements. Employers remain responsible for assessing and managing organisational causes of work-related stress, including demands, control, support, relationships, role and change.

See HSE work-related stress guidance and our professional standards.
