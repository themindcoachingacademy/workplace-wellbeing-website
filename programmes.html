How we build an effective programme

Invest in the right skills. Involve your people. Review what matters.

A workshop programme should give your organisation more than a full diary. It should focus investment on relevant needs, build shared skills and give you evidence to decide what to commission next.

01

Target the need — avoid wasted spend

We discuss your workforce challenges and existing support. Optional, voluntary employee feedback helps prioritise the topics people would actually find useful.

Employer benefit: a better-informed investment decision and a workshop programme connected to your people rather than a generic wellbeing calendar.

02

Build capability across teams

Employees take part in live group exercises, realistic workplace scenarios and practical skills practice. Sessions encourage constructive discussion without requiring anyone to disclose personal experiences.

Employer benefit: a shared language around pressure, stronger opportunities for collaboration and techniques colleagues can use after the session.

03

Review, adapt and build on progress

Agree suitable participation and anonymous feedback measures. Use the findings and the employer's priorities to plan follow-up workshops, different teams or longer-term provision.

Employer benefit: clearer visibility of uptake and relevance, helping you decide where future wellbeing investment is most useful.

Three levels of Clearer Mind

Choose the depth of support that fits your objectives and budget.

Every tier is live, interactive and non-clinical. Start with a practical introduction or commission a structured progression that allows more practice and reinforcement. Typical group size is up to 15 participants; larger workforces can be supported through multiple groups.

01

Introduction

One 2-hour workshop

Employees explore early signs of pressure, identify unhelpful thinking or emotional responses and practise an accessible technique to respond differently.

Employer benefit: broad initial reach, practical takeaways and a lower-commitment way to introduce wellbeing skills across a team.

02

Skills Development

Three 90-minute workshops

A progressive series covering automatic responses, overthinking and emotional pressure, with opportunities to practise between sessions and develop a seven-day skills plan.

Employer benefit: more time to engage, practise and consolidate useful approaches rather than relying on one isolated event.

03

Complete Clearer Mind

Six 90-minute workshops

A fuller programme that explores recurring patterns, confidence, beliefs, communication and personal influence, with repeated practice and an individual continuation plan.

Employer benefit: deeper learning, repeated opportunities to apply skills and a structured programme that can be reviewed over time.

The scope, schedule, venue and fee are quoted to suit each employer. These three tiers describe programme depth, not a rigid price list. Workshops support practical wellbeing skills; they do not substitute for medical care or management of workplace stressors.

Recurring workshop contracts

Continue the benefits across the year.

Once you have chosen the right content and depth, we can agree an ongoing delivery arrangement across departments, teams or sites — without forcing every employer into the same calendar.

Monthly delivery

Keep wellbeing learning visible with regular, practical sessions, rotating topics or groups as needed.

Employer value: a planned rhythm that can support consistent engagement and reinforce skills over time.

Quarterly workshops

Build a programme around four focused sessions or themed events each year, with time for application between workshops.

Employer value: predictable scheduling and spending with review points built in.

Multi-team or multi-site agreements

Deliver a consistent foundation to different teams, locations or shifts, adapting examples to each setting.

Employer value: coordinated provision across a larger workforce, with simpler planning and reporting.

These are contract formats, not additional Clearer Mind tiers. We will scope frequency, number of groups, location, evaluation and commercial terms before agreement.

Let employee feedback inform the topics

Ask your people what would actually help.

Instead of selecting topics solely from management assumptions, offer staff a short, voluntary workshop-priorities questionnaire. It asks what skills people would like to practise — such as managing pressure, confidence, communication or emotional regulation — without asking for diagnoses or personal disclosures.

The employer can use overall themes alongside existing staff feedback to help choose the most relevant workshops. Participation and individual answers should be handled with appropriate privacy safeguards.

What workshops can and cannot do

Workshops help employees develop skills. Employers also need to address the causes of workplace stress.

Our workshops help people practise responses to pressure, challenging situations and recurring patterns. However, individual skills cannot compensate for unreasonable workload, poor management, bullying or other work-related risks.

Employers are responsible for assessing and controlling workplace stress risks. Training can complement that work, but cannot replace it. We will be clear about this when recommending a programme.

Read HSE guidance on work-related stress or our professional standards.
