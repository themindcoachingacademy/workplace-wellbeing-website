Why workshops are different

1:1 support helps one person. Workshops improve shared capability across a group.

The purpose is not to deliver another wellbeing presentation. It is to help employees understand what pressure does to thinking and behaviour, recognise their own patterns earlier and practise more useful responses.

Prevention at scaleReach more employees before individual support is needed.
Earlier recognitionHelp people notice when stress, overthinking or emotional pressure is building rather than only responding after it becomes disruptive.
Practical self-managementGive employees tools they can use after the workshop rather than awareness alone.
Shared languageCreate a clearer, less clinical way for teams to talk about pressure, confidence and coping.
Better signpostingHelp employees understand when self-management is enough and when 1:1, Occupational Health, clinical or other support may be appropriate.
Targeted learningShape the workshop around the workforce issue rather than forcing a generic topic onto every employer.

03

Confidence & Resilience

Workshops sit inside a wider employer responsibility

HSE identifies six areas of work design linked to work-related stress.

Source: Health and Safety Executive.
01
Demands

Workload, work patterns and the working environment.

02
Control

How much say people have in the way they do their work.

03
Support

The resources, encouragement and help available from the employer and colleagues.

04–06
Relationships, role & change

The remaining HSE areas cover working relationships, role clarity and how organisational change is managed.

Workshops can strengthen awareness and individual capability, but they do not replace the employer's legal responsibility to assess and manage organisational causes of work-related stress.

The test of a useful workshop is not whether people enjoyed the hour. It is whether they leave knowing what to do differently when pressure shows up.
How the learning is structured

Recognise. Respond. Apply.

01Recognise the pattern

Understand triggers, thoughts, emotions and behaviours before the response becomes automatic.

02Respond differently

Practise strategies that create more choice under pressure.

03Apply it at work

Translate the learning into meetings, deadlines, difficult conversations, uncertainty and everyday demands.

What the employer can review

Workshops should produce more than attendance numbers.

Evaluation can be agreed around participation, anonymous feedback, perceived usefulness, confidence applying the learning and themes for future provision.

1

Participation
Who attended and overall engagement.

2

Anonymous feedback
Whether employees found the content relevant and useful.

3

Participant-reported change
Simple pre/post measures where appropriate.

4

Recommendations
Whether the workforce would benefit from follow-up learning or individual support routes.
