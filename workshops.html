Why live workshops are different

One-to-one support helps an individual. Interactive workshops help teams learn together.

Individual coaching gives an employee private space to work through personal difficulties. A live group workshop creates a different opportunity: colleagues can take part in shared learning, practise practical skills and explore everyday workplace situations together, without anyone being asked to disclose personal problems.

Rather than spending the session listening to a presentation, participants work through realistic examples, guided exercises and optional small-group discussion. That interaction can help employees better understand each other's perspectives, develop a shared language for pressure and communication, and practise responding more constructively.

Build stronger working relationshipsStructured group activities encourage listening, cooperation and understanding different perspectives in a low-pressure setting.
Improve communication across teamsEmployees practise how to respond in demanding conversations, manage misunderstandings and communicate with more awareness.
Create shared wellbeing skillsColleagues learn a common set of approaches to recognise pressure and choose more useful responses in day-to-day situations.
Encourage active participationPractical exercises and realistic scenarios help people experience techniques rather than simply hear about them.
Reach a group efficientlyOne workshop introduces skills to several employees at once, with options to repeat delivery across teams, locations or shifts.
Give learning a route into daily workParticipants identify how skills could be applied to meetings, deadlines, working relationships and changes in routine.

Participation is encouraged, never forced. Workshops are not group therapy and do not require disclosure of personal or medical information. Outcomes depend on context and should not be treated as guaranteed productivity or absence improvements.

Workshop themes

Start with what you want employees to handle better.

01

Stress, Pressure & Recovery

Recognise early signs of pressure, understand personal triggers and responses, and explore practical ways to manage stress and recover more effectively between periods of demand.

02

Anxiety, Overthinking & Mental Overload

Understand worry cycles and repetitive thinking, recognise what keeps them going and practise ways of interrupting unhelpful patterns.

03

Confidence & Resilience at Work

Develop more constructive responses to setbacks, uncertainty and challenging situations while building confidence in handling everyday workplace pressures.

04

Emotional Regulation at Work

Understand how emotional responses can influence thoughts, behaviour and communication, and practise ways of creating more choice before automatically reacting.

05

Communication & Difficult Conversations

Explore rapport, communication, emotional responses and practical ways of approaching conversations that might otherwise be avoided or become unnecessarily difficult.

06

Changing Unhelpful Reactions & Patterns

Help employees recognise automatic patterns, understand what triggers them and explore practical ways of responding differently when those patterns appear.

Before delivery, we discuss your workforce, the pressures or challenges you are seeing and what you want employees to take away from the session. Workshops can then be tailored around the audience and desired learning outcomes rather than delivering the same generic wellbeing presentation to every organisation.

Workshops sit inside a wider employer responsibility

The six HSE Management Standards areas linked to work-related stress.

Source: Health and Safety Executive.

Demands

02
Control

How much say people have in the way they do their work.

03
Support

The resources, encouragement and help available from the employer and colleagues.

Whether people understand their role and whether conflicting roles are avoided.

Change

How organisational change is managed and communicated.

Workshops can strengthen awareness and individual capability, but they do not replace the employer's legal responsibility to assess and manage organisational causes of work-related stress.

The test of a useful workshop is not whether people enjoyed the hour. It is whether they leave knowing what to do differently when pressure shows up.

Workplace wellbeing workshop FAQs

Planning a workshop for your organisation.

How long is a workplace wellbeing workshop?Our three Clearer Mind tiers are one 2-hour introductory workshop, three 90-minute skills-development workshops, or six 90-minute workshops for the complete programme. We agree scheduling and any tailored alternatives during commissioning.
Can you tailor a workshop to our organisation?Yes. We will discuss what you are seeing within the workforce, who will be attending and what you want employees to understand or handle better. The workshop can then be shaped around those objectives.
Are the workshops delivered face to face?Yes. Face-to-face delivery is our primary format. Sessions are live and interactive, giving employees opportunities to participate and practise skills together.
Can workshops also be delivered online?Yes. Live online workshops are available where this better suits the workforce, including teams working across several locations. In-person delivery across the North West remains our main focus.
Are workshops suitable for managers?Yes. Sessions can be developed for employees, managers or specific teams. Manager-focused workshops can also explore communication, difficult conversations, emotional responses and supporting healthier workplace interactions.
Are the workshops therapy or mental-health treatment?No. The workshops provide practical, non-clinical wellbeing education and skills development. They are not therapy, diagnosis or a substitute for appropriate medical, psychological, Occupational Health or emergency support.
Use workshops when the need is broad

What do you want a group of employees to understand or handle better?

Tell us the workforce challenge, audience and desired learning outcome. The workshop can then be built around that result rather than a generic wellbeing presentation.
