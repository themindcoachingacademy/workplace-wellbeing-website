Professional standards & governance

Know the boundaries before you trust a provider with your employees.

For an employer, confidentiality is only useful when it is clearly defined. This page sets out the scope, safeguarding, reporting and escalation principles behind the workplace service.

What the organisation can expect

Six areas should be clear before support begins.

01

Confidentiality

Individual session content is confidential subject to safeguarding, serious-risk and legal exceptions. Routine session content is not reported to the employer.

02

Scope of Practice

The service provides non-clinical coaching and wellbeing support. It does not replace medical diagnosis, psychotherapy, psychiatric care or emergency mental-health services.

03

Safeguarding

Where significant risk or safeguarding concerns arise, appropriate escalation may be necessary and the relevant route is followed.

04

Record Keeping

Sessions are not routinely recorded. Brief, proportionate confidential notes are retained securely with access restricted to the practitioner.

05

Organisational Reporting

Reporting can include agreed utilisation, attendance and broad non-identifying themes — not individual session notes or routine confidential disclosures.

06

Complaints

Formal complaints are handled through a documented process, with a substantive response target of 10 working days.

The employer benefit of clear boundaries

Employees know what is private. Employers know what happens when risk changes.

That clarity supports trust in the service while avoiding ambiguity about safeguarding, serious risk, clinical needs or organisational reporting.

1

Before launch
Access, eligibility, confidentiality, data and escalation arrangements are agreed.

2

During delivery
Individual content remains confidential within the defined exceptions.

3

When risk requires action
The appropriate safeguarding, emergency or clinical route is used.

4

At review
The organisation receives only the level of non-identifying information agreed.

A useful procurement question

“What exactly will you tell us about an employee — and what will you not tell us?”

A credible provider should be able to answer that before the service starts. The principle here is simple: the employer receives what it needs to understand provision and risk, without turning confidential support into another management-reporting channel.
