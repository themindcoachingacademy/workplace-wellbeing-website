The employer benefit

A clinic makes support visible, accessible and operationally simple.

A remote service can be useful, but some workforces respond better when support is physically present. A clinic day gives employees a clear opportunity to access help during the working environment while giving the employer a defined capacity and predictable structure.

Reduce access frictionNo travel to an external venue and no need for an employee to find a provider themselves.
Make wellbeing support visibleA regular clinic signals that support is part of the organisation's practical wellbeing provision, not just a policy document.
Protect manager timeManagers can signpost to a professional route instead of repeatedly holding conversations they may not be trained to manage.
Create predictable capacityMorning, afternoon and full-day blocks make it easier to budget, communicate and review utilisation.

What the organisation provides

A private room and a clear route for employees to access it.

  • A room where conversations cannot reasonably be overheard
  • A named administrative contact
  • Agreed employee access arrangements
  • A safeguarding or escalation contact where appropriate
Private self-booking

Employees book available clinic slots directly.

The organisation can receive agreed utilisation information without receiving confidential session content.

Unused appointments remain part of the booked clinic block because the practitioner time has been reserved specifically for the organisation.

How clinics differ from remote 1:1 support

The clinic is about presence and access.

Remote 1:1 support is flexible and can work UK-wide. The clinic model is different: support is physically present, capacity is reserved for a defined day, and employees can access it within the working environment.
