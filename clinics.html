Why employers use clinics

Sometimes the problem is not lack of support. It is lack of access.

Employees may know support exists and still not use it because it feels distant, inconvenient, unclear or difficult to access during the working week. An on-site clinic removes several of those barriers at once.

Increase visibilityEmployees know when and where confidential support is available.
Reduce frictionNo external travel, no searching for a provider and no need for management to arrange every conversation.
Create predictable capacityThe employer knows exactly how much practitioner time is reserved and can review whether it is being used.
Protect manager timeManagers gain a practical signposting route when an employee needs more than a normal workplace conversation.
Demonstrate visible investmentA regular clinic turns wellbeing provision into something employees can see and access rather than a benefit that exists only on paper.
Generate useful service-level insightReporting can show utilisation, attendance and broad non-identifying themes without disclosing private conversations.

What you provide

A private room and a clear employee access route.

  • A room where conversations cannot reasonably be overheard
  • A named administrative contact
  • Agreed access and internal communication arrangements
  • A safeguarding or escalation contact where appropriate
Private self-booking

Employees book available clinic slots directly.

This keeps unnecessary management involvement out of individual appointments while allowing the employer to review overall use of the clinic.

Unused appointments remain part of the booked clinic block because practitioner time has been reserved specifically for the employer.
