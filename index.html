If I were the employer

I would want to know one thing first: what does this improve for my people and my organisation?

The service should earn its place by making support easier to access, helping employees address problems earlier and reducing the amount of emotional pressure that is left to line managers, HR or absence processes to deal with later.

Support people before absence escalatesGive employees somewhere to go while they are still functioning at work, not only once they reach crisis point.
Protect concentration and performanceStress and persistent worry can affect focus, communication, motivation and decision-making long before somebody goes off sick.
Reduce pressure on managers and HRProvide a professional route for issues that need more than a supportive management conversation.
Improve employee confidence in seeking helpAn independent route can feel easier to use when the employee does not want every personal difficulty entering the management chain.
Strengthen retention and employee experienceEmployees are more likely to feel supported when wellbeing provision is visible, accessible and practical.
Add structure without replacing existing provisionThe service can sit alongside HR, Occupational Health, EAP and clinical pathways rather than compete with them.

Why independence matters

Some employees will speak more openly outside the management chain.

That does not mean excluding the employer. It means separating confidential support from routine performance management, while keeping safeguarding, escalation and appropriate organisational reporting clearly defined.

What good provision should feel like

Easy to understand. Easy to access. Safe to use.

Employees should know what the service is for, how to access it and what remains confidential. Employers should know what they are commissioning, what it complements and how it will be reviewed.
